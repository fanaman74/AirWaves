# Punch-ins on a talking head

On a single-camera talking head, move between the wide framing and a tighter one so jump cuts don't jump and the lines that matter land closer.

## Where to punch in

- Where two pieces of the same shot meet at a cut, change the framing across the cut so it reads as a new angle instead of a jump. Inside a sentence, a mild step in is all the cut needs, and the framing doesn't pull back out on a later cut while the sentence carries on: come back out where the thought ends. Cuts that come close together can share the tight framing. Where the cut took out a long wait, the speaker often comes back in a different position and with less energy: give that incoming line the tighter framing, so the cut moves toward them, not away.
- Push in on a line that deserves weight, such as a punchline, a key claim, a reveal or a strong reaction, and come back out when that thought ends.
- Hold each framing for at least a few seconds. Punching in on every sentence turns into a rhythm the viewer notices.

## The framing

- A punch-in across a jump cut only needs to read as a new angle: about 110 percent of the wide frame as a guide. A line that deserves weight can go to about 120 percent; go further only for a deliberate close-up.
- Keep the eyes about a third down from the top, with a little more room on the side the speaker looks toward. Only a close-up loses the top of the head; the chin and mouth always stay in.
- Place each framing from where the face actually is through that stretch, not from one frame, so a lean or a turn doesn't take the face out of frame.
- The framing changes on the cut, or on a beat between sentences, never mid-word. It is a cut, not a move. Use a slow push only when the editor asks for one.
- Never let a framing flash by. Where an insert or a screen demo hands back to the camera on or near a cut that changes the framing, make both changes on the same frame, so the wide shot doesn't play for a moment between the insert and the punch-in. A framing on screen for well under a second reads as a glitch.
- Keep within what the source can hold. A 4K source punched into a 1080p edit stays sharp up to about twice the size; an HD source softens past a small punch-in.

A camera shown as an inset over a screen recording stays as it is. Framing the screen is a separate job.
